Kolkata’s summers are getting harsher, and the heat does not hurt everyone the same. An old person living alone, a small child in a tin-roof room, a family with no fan: they feel it first, and they feel it most.

S.H.A.D.E. is a prototype of a concept. It explores where a small heat budget should go, so it reaches these people first and not just the reddest dots on a map.

About S.H.A.D.E.

A heat map only shows where it is hottest. But on the hottest night, the people in danger are the old, the very young, and families under tin roofs. S.H.A.D.E. is a prototype of a concept for making the money reach them first.

Prototype of a concept · Kolkata only for now · every number is an estimate from a model, not a measured result

Read this before you trust the numbers
  • Old ward map. The ward outlines come from an older 141-ward KMC map. KMC’s current map has 144 wards, so some ward numbers and borders may not match today’s.
  • Old and modelled people data. Population and age groups are WorldPop estimates, not a headcount. Census 2011 is more than a decade old, so it is not used for headcounts.
  • Assumed housing figures. The slum share and poor-roof share of each ward are assumed values, not Census counts, because no ward-level table was available.
  • Heat, not health. Temperatures are from satellite pictures of the ground surface. They are not how hot people feel, and the plan has not been checked against heat illness or hospital data.
  • Walking distance. Measured along real streets (OpenStreetMap) only for water points. Other relief numbers are estimates.
Why this approach? Three short reasons
Hottest is not always neediest If we only chase the hottest spot, we may cool an empty warehouse roof while a crowded bustee a few wards away bakes inside. So we also look at who lives there: old people, small kids, tin roofs.
One budget, two pots One pot builds things that stay: cool roofs and trees. The other pot helps this summer: water points, cool rooms, ORS at schools. Move the slider and see what you give up.
Do not just trust us. Check. The numbers come from 50 models, not one, so you can see how sure we are. You can ask for the careful version too. Where we could measure it, walking distance follows real streets, not neat circles. Other relief numbers are marked as estimates.
